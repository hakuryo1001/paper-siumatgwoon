\section{Opposites}

It is very natural for us to think of "opposite" qualities adhering to a concept. Intuitively, we'd think that qualities, attributes, predicates, properties, are "opposite" or "opposed" to each other if they cannot coexist or simultaneously adhere to the same object. In other words, we'd think that qualities are opposites if they are mutually exclusive. An object can be red, or not red, or neither red nor not red, but it cannot be simultaneously be red and not red.

Given our natural tendencies and naturally enabled capability to speak of "not-something", it is very attractive to speak of the opposites or negations of the objects in a siumatgwoon. Obviously, a great deal of our intuition and confidence in speaking about "nots" are anchored in the infrastructure granted to us in propositional logic. But we are talking about negations of objects in a logic of metaphysics. It is to talk about "not" things. It is to talk about not apples, not red, not great. But are they really things? 

What is the opposite of "up"? "Down" clearly - as anyone would immediately reply. But "right", "left", "forward", "backward" are also "not-up"s.
